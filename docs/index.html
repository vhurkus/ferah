A Mac cleaner that shows its work

Ferah uninstalls apps with all their leftovers, shows what fills your disk and clears System Data. It explains why each file is safe to remove, and never deletes anything permanently: everything goes to the Trash.

Everything a Mac cleaner should do

And nothing it shouldn't: no fake speed boosts, no memory "cleaning", no removing language files from apps.

Uninstall apps completely

Dragging an app to the Trash leaves its data behind. Ferah finds it all.

  • Containers, caches, preferences, saved state, scripts and crash reports in ~/Library
  • Launch agents, daemons and helper tools in /Library
  • Only files that provably belong to the app start checked; guesses are left for you
  • Notices apps you drag to the Trash yourself and offers their leftovers
  • Finds leftovers of apps you removed long ago
  • Sort apps by size or by when you last used them

See what fills your disk

Your home folder, apps, /Library and Homebrew, measured in one pass.

  • Open any folder to see what's inside, largest first
  • Move anything to the Trash right from the list
  • The part macOS keeps for itself is shown separately, never touched

Install Ferah

Two ways, both free. Ferah is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens without warnings.

  1. With Homebrew

    If you use Homebrew, one command installs Ferah and brew upgrade keeps it up to date:

    brew install --cask vhurkus/tap/ferah

  2. Or download the DMG

    Get the latest Ferah-x.y.z.dmg from GitHub Releases, open it and drag Ferah to Applications.

  3. Give Ferah Full Disk Access (recommended)

    Without it macOS hides some folders, so sizes come out smaller than they are. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access and turn on Ferah. Ferah's overview walks you through it and reopens itself when you're done.

  4. Allow the prompts when they come up

    macOS may ask to let Ferah control Finder (used for items that need your password, like apps from the App Store) and to send notifications (used when an app you trash leaves files behind, or when space runs low).

  5. Scan

    Press Scan (⌘R). Nothing is removed until you check it and confirm. Removed items land in the Trash; space is freed when you empty it.

To uninstall Ferah itself: brew uninstall --cask ferah, or drag it to the Trash.

Questions

Is Ferah free?

Yes. Ferah is free and open source under the MIT license, with no subscriptions, ads or in-app purchases.

Does Ferah delete files permanently?

No. Everything Ferah removes goes to the Trash, where Finder's Put Back still works. Space is freed when you empty the Trash, which Ferah only does when you ask and confirm.

How do I completely uninstall an app on Mac?

Dragging an app to the Trash leaves its files in ~/Library. Pick the app in Ferah's Applications and it lists the app's containers, caches, preferences, saved state, launch agents and files in /Library, preselecting those that provably belong to it. If you already dragged an app to the Trash, Ferah notices and offers what it left behind.

What is System Data on Mac, and how do I reduce it?

System Data includes Time Machine local snapshots, Xcode simulator runtimes, iPhone and iPad backups, caches and Messages attachments. Ferah's System Data view lists the parts you can act on and removes each one with the tool macOS expects: tmutil for snapshots, simctl for simulators, the Trash for backups.

Is it safe?

Every removal passes one policy you can read in the source: nothing outside your home folder except third-party apps and their leftovers in /Library, nothing in Keychains, iCloud Drive, Mail or Messages, nothing that belongs to macOS or Apple's apps. Items you can't move yourself go through Finder, which asks for your password.

Why isn't Ferah on the Mac App Store?

Mac App Store apps must run in a sandbox and can't ask for administrator rights, so they can't remove apps' leftovers or measure the whole disk. Ferah is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple instead.
